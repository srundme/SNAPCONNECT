MATERIALS FOR FURNITURE AND CABINETS
- Plywood is the usual base for kitchens and wardrobes. BWP (boiling water proof) or marine grade suits kitchens, sink areas and bathrooms because it handles moisture. MR (moisture resistant) grade is common for wardrobes, TV units and dry areas.
- MDF and HDHMR boards are smooth and good for shutters, routed designs and painted finishes. HDHMR handles moisture better than MDF. Particle board is the budget option and is best kept away from water.
- Finishes on shutters: laminate is tough, easy to clean and comes in many colours and textures, including wood and stone looks. Acrylic gives a high gloss mirror-like look and suits modern kitchens. PU paint gives a seamless matte or gloss finish in any colour. Veneer is real wood and gives a warm, premium look but needs more care. Membrane (PVC foil) suits curved or routed shutters.
- Edge banding on every exposed edge stops moisture getting into the board.
- Hardware matters as much as the board: soft-close hinges, telescopic or tandem drawer channels, lift-up systems for upper kitchen cabinets and good handles or handle-less profiles decide how it feels every day.

MODULAR KITCHEN
- Layouts: L-shape suits most apartment kitchens and keeps the work triangle tight. Parallel (galley) suits long narrow kitchens. U-shape gives the most counter and storage for bigger kitchens. Straight (single wall) suits small or open kitchens. An island or breakfast counter needs open space around it.
- Work triangle: keep the sink, hob and fridge close but not cramped so cooking is easy.
- Storage ideas: tall unit or pantry pull-out, corner carousel or magic corner, tandem drawers for vessels, bottle pull-out near the hob, cutlery and thali organisers, a separate unit for the mixer and appliances, dustbin pull-out under the sink.
- Countertops: granite is tough and heat resistant and very common. Quartz is non-porous, uniform and stain resistant. Marble looks beautiful but stains and etches easily in Indian cooking.
- A chimney above the hob and enough plug points at the counter make a big daily difference. Backsplash tiles or a quartz backsplash keep cleaning easy.
- Many Indian homes like a separate wet or utility area for washing vessels and heavy cooking.

WARDROBES AND STORAGE
- Sliding wardrobes save space in front and suit smaller bedrooms. Hinged wardrobes open fully and are easier to organise. A walk-in wardrobe needs a dedicated space.
- Inside: hanging for long and short clothes, shelves, drawers, a locker, trouser and tie pull-outs, a loft for seasonal storage, and a mirror on a shutter or inside.
- Loft cabinets above wardrobes add storage for suitcases and rarely used items.
- Smart storage: beds with hydraulic storage, seating with storage, wall-mounted units to keep floors clear, shoe racks near the entrance, crockery units in the dining area.

LIVING AND BEDROOMS
- TV unit: a wall-mounted unit with a panel behind keeps wires hidden and makes the wall a feature. Leave space for a set-top box, router and speakers.
- Bedrooms: a padded or panelled headboard wall, side tables, a study or dressing unit where space allows, and blackout curtains for good sleep.
- Kids rooms: durable laminates, rounded edges, flexible storage that grows with the child, a study table with good light.
- Study or work-from-home corner: a deep desk, closed storage, task lighting and plug points at desk height.

POOJA SPACE
- Pooja units can be wall-mounted, floor-standing or a small dedicated room. Common materials are wood, MDF with CNC jali designs, and marble or Corian looks. Backlit jali panels and bells or diya ledges are popular.
- Many families prefer the pooja space in the north-east and facing east, following vastu.

FALSE CEILING AND LIGHTING
- Gypsum board ceilings are smooth, quick and widely used. POP allows more detailed shapes. Wooden or PVC panels add warmth in places.
- Cove lighting inside a ceiling gives soft indirect light. Recessed spotlights or COB lights add focus.
- Plan light in layers: general light for the room, task light for kitchen counters, study and mirrors, and accent light for art, shelves and TV walls.
- Warm white (around 2700K to 3000K) feels cosy for living and bedrooms. Neutral or cool white (4000K and above) suits kitchens and study areas.
- Profile lights inside wardrobes and under kitchen wall cabinets are practical and look premium.

WALLS, FLOORS AND FINISHES
- Wall options: paint, textured paint, wallpaper, wall panels in wood, fluted or slatted designs, and stone or tile cladding for a feature wall.
- A floral or patterned mural or wallpaper works best on one feature wall, balanced with plain walls and simple furniture.
- Floors: vitrified tiles are common and easy to maintain. Wooden or laminate flooring adds warmth to bedrooms. Marble and granite suit a premium living area.

STYLES
- Modern: clean lines, flat shutters, handle-less profiles, neutral colours with one bold accent.
- Contemporary: current trends, mixed textures, fluted panels, matte finishes, metal accents.
- Minimal: very few, functional pieces, lots of closed storage, calm colours.
- Scandinavian: light woods, white and soft grey, cosy textiles, simple shapes.
- Japandi: a mix of Japanese and Scandinavian, natural wood, low furniture, earthy tones, very calm.
- Indian traditional or ethnic: carved wood, brass, jali work, rich colours, a strong pooja space.
- Luxury or neo-classical: mouldings, marble looks, gold or brass details, statement lighting.
- Boho or earthy: woven pendant lights, rattan and cane, plants, warm terracotta and floral patterns.

SPACE PLANNING TIPS
- In a 2 BHK or 3 BHK apartment, wall-to-wall wardrobes, beds with storage and lofts free up floor space.
- Light colours and mirrors make small rooms feel bigger. Dark accents work best on one wall.
- Leave at least about 3 feet of walking space around beds and in front of wardrobes and kitchen counters.
- Plan electrical points and lights with the furniture layout, before civil work and painting.
- A ready home can start design right away. For an under-construction home, finalising the design before possession helps the work start sooner after handover.

VASTU BASICS (ONLY IF THEY ASK OR CARE ABOUT IT)
- Kitchen in the south-east is commonly preferred, with the cook facing east.
- Master bedroom in the south-west, with the bed's head towards south or west.
- Pooja in the north-east.
- Keep the north-east light and uncluttered.
Never insist on vastu. Follow what the family prefers.

HOW TO USE THIS IN CHAT
- Connect a suggestion to their situation, for example a parallel kitchen for a long narrow space, sliding wardrobes for a smaller bedroom, or cove lighting to match the warm mood in their photo.
- Offer one idea at a time and ask what they like. The designer finalises everything in the consultation.
- Never give prices, brand names, warranties or timelines from this section.
